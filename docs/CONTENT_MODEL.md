# Content Model

Patient-facing pages should be structured so humans and AI can both understand what is approved, what is uncertain, and where claims came from.

## Recommended page sections

1. **Plain-language summary** — what this topic is and why it matters.
2. **What we know** — reviewed evidence, with citations.
3. **What depends on the individual situation** — important modifiers and applicability limits.
4. **What remains uncertain or disputed** — gaps/conflicts.
5. **Community experience** — clearly labeled qualitative/aggregate observations, never mixed into evidence claims.
6. **Questions to discuss with a clinician/PT** — decision-preparation, not prescribed decisions.
7. **Sources and review status** — provenance, version, last reviewed.

## Metadata

Knowledge pages should eventually expose machine-readable metadata including:
- topic/condition;
- journey stage;
- intended audience;
- evidence records used;
- review class/status;
- medical reviewer(s) where applicable;
- last-reviewed date;
- languages available;
- known evidence gaps.

## Writing standard

Prefer plain language, short paragraphs, concrete explanations, definitions of unavoidable technical terms, and explicit distinctions between possibility and certainty.

Avoid:
- “always/never” unless a reliable source truly supports it;
- generic success percentages without population/context;
- treatment rankings based on anecdotes;
- false precision;
- implying a guideline covers populations that it explicitly excludes.

## Single-source rule

Translations and website renderings should derive from one canonical content object/page where possible. Do not manually maintain multiple conflicting copies of the same medical claim.
