# Moderation Principles

Moderation protects people, evidence quality, and the usefulness of the commons.

## Remove or restrict
- identifiable patient data posted without an approved process;
- harassment, doxxing, threats, spam, or undisclosed commercial promotion;
- fabricated citations or knowingly false medical claims;
- individualized prescriptions presented as community guidance;
- attempts to use popularity or credentials to bypass review requirements.

## Do not suppress merely because
- a patient experience differs from common experience;
- a credible source conflicts with another credible source;
- a contributor questions an established recommendation with a traceable source;
- someone reports an unfavorable outcome.

## Medical disagreement
Moderators should redirect disputes toward claim scope, source quality, applicability, certainty, and conflicts of interest. The goal is not consensus at any cost; unresolved disagreement may be documented explicitly.

## Commercial content
References to products, clinics, surgeons, devices, or services require special caution. Promotional claims and undisclosed incentives are not acceptable. Future directories/reviews, if built, need separate methodology and governance.

## Appeals
As the community grows, moderation decisions affecting contributor access or removal of substantive content should have a documented appeal path and audit trail.
