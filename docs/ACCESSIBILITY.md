# Accessibility and Inclusion

Accessibility is a product requirement, not a later polish step.

## Default UX expectations
- mobile-first layouts;
- plain language before specialist terminology;
- adequate text size and spacing;
- keyboard-accessible controls;
- semantic headings and form labels;
- captions/transcripts for audiovisual material;
- do not rely on color alone to communicate risk or evidence status;
- readable tables with a non-table alternative when needed;
- alt text for meaningful images/diagrams.

## Cognitive accessibility
People may arrive anxious, in pain, recently diagnosed, or unfamiliar with medical systems. Prefer progressive disclosure: concise answer first, detail and evidence below.

## Health literacy
Explain unavoidable terms in everyday language. Do not make understanding dependent on knowing acronyms such as ACLR, RTS, ROM, or RCT.

## Global inclusion
Do not assume access to a specific insurance system, specialist, imaging modality, physiotherapy frequency, expensive device, paid AI service, or high-speed connection.

Accessibility testing should become part of the website release checklist before public launch.
