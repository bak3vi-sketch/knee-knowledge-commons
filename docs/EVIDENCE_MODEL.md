# Evidence Model

The project must distinguish **where a statement comes from** from **how certain we are that it is true**.

## Evidence/source types
Use explicit labels such as:
- clinical guideline;
- systematic review / meta-analysis;
- randomized trial;
- observational study;
- diagnostic/prognostic study;
- expert consensus / position statement;
- clinical interpretation;
- patient/community experience.

A source type is not automatically a certainty rating.

## Certainty
When a body of evidence has been formally assessed with GRADE or another documented framework, record the framework and rating. For GRADE, use the standard levels: **high, moderate, low, very low**.

Do not assign a formal GRADE rating merely because an individual paper is an RCT or observational study. Formal certainty assessment belongs to a body of evidence for a defined outcome/question.

If no formal assessment has been performed, use `not_assessed` rather than guessing.

## Minimum claim record
A medically meaningful claim should be traceable to:
- claim text;
- topic and population/context;
- source(s);
- source type;
- publication date/version where relevant;
- key limitations;
- certainty framework/rating if formally assessed;
- reviewer and review status;
- date last verified.

## Evidence hierarchy is not a truth hierarchy
A higher-level design can still be poorly conducted or irrelevant to a particular population. Applicability, risk of bias, indirectness, inconsistency, imprecision, publication bias, and outcome relevance matter.

## Community experience
Lived experience can answer questions that trials often do not capture well, such as:
- what people wish they had known;
- communication failures;
- barriers to rehabilitation;
- common fears and practical difficulties;
- questions patients found useful;
- where the care journey feels confusing.

It cannot by itself establish that one treatment is more effective or safer than another.

## Conflicts and gaps
Approved knowledge should preserve:
- conflicting recommendations;
- outdated evidence with historical relevance;
- evidence gaps;
- populations not represented by available research;
- outcomes important to patients but poorly studied.

The goal is not to make every answer look certain. The goal is to make the state of knowledge legible.
