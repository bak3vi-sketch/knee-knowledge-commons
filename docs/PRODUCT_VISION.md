# Product Vision

## Mission
Help people with knee injuries understand their situation, avoid preventable information mistakes, and participate more effectively in healthcare decisions by connecting trustworthy evidence with clearly labeled lived experience.

## Launch strategy — Vietnam-first, global-ready
The first product market is Vietnam. Early user research, patient-facing content, contribution workflows, terminology, and usability testing should focus on real needs in Vietnam.

This does **not** mean building a Vietnam-only architecture. Shared medical evidence, evidence records, identifiers, provenance, schemas, AI-provider interfaces, and governance should remain reusable internationally. Vietnam-specific care pathways, insurance/access assumptions, terminology, and local service context must be modeled as local context rather than universal medical truth.

The project should earn the right to expand internationally by first proving that one country can use the complete workflow safely and usefully.

## Long-term product
A multilingual, mobile-first public website where a person can:
- navigate by their stage of the knee-injury journey;
- search and ask questions in everyday language;
- see the sources, limits, and uncertainty behind answers;
- compare options without being told what individualized treatment to choose;
- learn from aggregate community experience without mistaking anecdotes for proof;
- prepare useful questions for appointments and rehabilitation sessions;
- suggest corrections or contribute knowledge without learning GitHub.

Clinicians and researchers should be able to submit sources, corrections, interpretation, and review through similarly convenient web workflows. GitHub remains available as an expert/maintainer path and canonical version history.

## Initial Vietnam product principle
For ordinary users in Vietnam, Vietnamese is the primary interface and patient-facing content language. Technical implementation, research sources, and canonical machine-readable structures may remain English where that improves interoperability.

Internal project documentation does not need a dedicated Vietnamese mirror when browser/AI translation is sufficient. When the project owner needs to make a decision, agents should explain the decision and trade-offs in Vietnamese and plain language.

## Product values
1. **Useful before impressive.** Optimize for better understanding and fewer avoidable mistakes, not AI novelty.
2. **Traceable before fluent.** A beautifully written answer without support is worse than a cautious sourced answer.
3. **Patient-friendly, not patient-directive.** Empower decisions without replacing individualized clinical judgment.
4. **Community-rich, evidence-aware.** Respect lived experience while preserving epistemic boundaries.
5. **Privacy by design.** Never make users trade unnecessary exposure for usefulness.
6. **Vietnam-first, global by architecture.** Validate locally; structure evidence, data, and systems so international expansion is localization rather than rebuilding.
7. **Model-agnostic.** AI providers change faster than the project's mission, data model, and community.
8. **Owner-comprehensible.** Project decisions must be explainable in Vietnamese and plain language; implementation complexity should not become a barrier to governance.

## Success measures
During the Vietnam pilot, measure whether users can understand key concepts, find traceable sources, identify uncertainty, prepare better questions, report fewer information dead ends, and contribute safely. Raw chat volume or AI answer count is not the primary measure of success.

International expansion is a later success condition, not an initial vanity metric.
