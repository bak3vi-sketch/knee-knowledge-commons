# Plan 0002 — ACL + Meniscus Knowledge Pilot

Status: Planned

## Goal
Prove the complete evidence-to-patient-content workflow on ACL and meniscus topics before expanding to the rest of the knee.

## Why this scope
ACL and meniscus injuries are common, frequently overlap, contain important treatment/rehabilitation decision points, and expose the danger of applying evidence outside its intended population.

## Workstreams
1. Build a source inventory: guidelines, systematic reviews, key trials/observational evidence, and relevant patient-education resources.
2. Define core questions by patient journey rather than by anatomy alone.
3. Create structured evidence records with scope, provenance, limitations, review state, and certainty where formally assessed.
4. Draft plain-language pages from reviewed records.
5. Run citation-fidelity and contradiction checks.
6. Obtain qualified human review for Class C claims.
7. Record unresolved questions and evidence gaps.

## Initial content questions
- What does an ACL/meniscus diagnosis mean?
- What associated injuries change the conversation?
- What treatment paths exist and what factors commonly influence decision-making?
- What is prehabilitation?
- How is rehabilitation commonly staged and assessed?
- What does “return to running/sport” mean and why can criteria matter?
- What information do patients commonly wish they had earlier?

These are research questions for knowledge building, not pre-written conclusions.

## Non-goals
- recommending surgery/non-surgery to an individual;
- building a universal rehab protocol;
- comparing clinicians or hospitals;
- using raw patient stories as treatment-effectiveness evidence.

## Acceptance criteria
- Every important patient-facing medical claim is traceable to reviewed evidence.
- Scope exclusions are visible.
- Conflicting/uncertain evidence is preserved.
- ACL+meniscus combined injuries are not silently treated as equivalent to isolated ACL or isolated meniscus populations.
- At least one complete user journey can be rendered from canonical knowledge.
